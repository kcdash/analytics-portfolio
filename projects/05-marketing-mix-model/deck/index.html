Why

Media is planned around the calendar, so the calendar can explain the media

How much of each channel's pattern the calendar already explains

R-squared of each channel's saturated pressure on the trend, season, holiday and price terms.

Flyer drops are scheduled around the same peaks the model already controls for. of the flyer variable is reproducible from the calendar alone, so the model cannot tell whether December sales came from the flyer or from December.

TV is the opposite: it runs in flights that cut across the season, so only of it is calendar. It is the one channel the data can speak to.

This is the test that should decide where to spend research money: test the channels the calendar can imitate, not the ones with the biggest budgets.

Budget scale

On this quarter's margin alone, the media budget roughly breaks even

Return on the media budget, by budget level

Incremental gross margin per dollar of media, at the best allocation for each budget level, on the true curves. One is break-even.

At today's budget, media returns of gross margin per dollar spent in the same period. Cutting the budget raises that ratio and lowers total profit; adding to it does the reverse.

That is the wrong frame on its own. Media also recruits customers, and the acquisition work in this portfolio values a new customer at $428 of first-year margin, with more beyond it.

Judge the mix on the immediate return, which is what this model measures well once calibrated. Judge the scale on lifetime value, which needs the acquisition and retention models, not this one.

Appendix

Method, assumptions and limits

Model. Weekly sales = base x (1 + sum of channel lifts). The base is log-linear in trend, three annual harmonics, holiday and back-to-school flags, and realised price against list. Channel lift is a Hill saturation of geometrically adstocked spend. Channel effects are constrained to be positive.

Transforms. Carry-over (nine values), saturation shape (five) and half-saturation point (seven) searched per channel by coordinate descent on four expanding-window folds.

Prior. Without evidence to the contrary the split of contribution is pulled gently toward the split of spend, which stops the fit collapsing onto one channel.

Tests. Holdouts are simulated from the true response curves with the sampling error a geo test of thirteen weeks would carry. In a real engagement these are read off the test, not generated.

Limits. Weekly national data cannot see competitor activity, distribution changes or creative quality, none of which exist in this dataset either. Price is controlled but not modelled as a response. The optimiser assumes steady weekly spend and ignores flighting, minimum buys and contractual commitments.

Not tested. Interaction between channels, long-term brand effects beyond the carry-over window, and the effect of media on the customer base rather than on the week's sales.

Synthetic data. Maple & Main is fictional. Its media response curves are known, which is the only reason the accuracy claims above can be made at all. Figures illustrate the method, not any real company.
