Test design

A 10% holdout for two quarters settles it

Smallest effect the test can detect, in conversion points
Holdout shareOne quarterTwo quartersOne year

80% power, 5% two-sided significance, at prospects a year and a conversion rate. In brackets: the smallest share of conversions caused by outreach that the test would detect.

  • Design: hold back a random 10% of prospects from outreach for 26 weeks. Compare 45-day conversion.
  • What it answers: whether outreach causes at least of conversions, and how that varies by score band.
  • Cost: about prospects go uncontacted. If outreach is fully effective, that forgoes roughly of first-year margin, less the contact cost saved.
  • Decision rule: expand outreach to every score band whose measured incremental value exceeds the contact cost.

Appendix

Method, assumptions and limits

Population. Prospects first contacted from 1 January 2024 to 15 September 2026. Later prospects are excluded because conversion can take 45 days.

Target. Became a loyalty member within 45 days of first contact.

Validation. Trained on prospects to September 2025, tested on from the following year.

Scorecard. Unregularised logistic regression on lead source, age band, income band, web visits (capped at 8), distance, children and email engagement.

Look-alike index. For each trait, the share of converters with it divided by the share of all prospects with it; a prospect's score is the product of its indices.

Customer value. Mean first-year margin of converted prospects with a full year of orders. never ordered and are included at zero.

Assumed, not measured. Outreach cost of per prospect, and cost per lead by source. Both can be changed in the simulator.

Not known. How many conversions outreach causes. All "extra customer" figures compare ranked and random lists of contacted prospects and assume outreach is what converts them.

Limits. First-year margin understates lifetime value. Higher-scoring prospects become slightly more valuable customers (about 9% between bottom and top third); a single average value is used.

Synthetic data. Maple & Main is fictional. The data was generated with a known conversion rule, which is why the model can be compared with the truth. Figures illustrate the method, not any real company.

Code. SQL, Python and SAS, with a model card and QC log, are in the project repository.
