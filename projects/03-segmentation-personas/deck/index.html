Appendix

Method, assumptions and limits

Population. Customers of at least a year's standing with two or more orders in the twelve months before the snapshot: at May 2026.

Personas. K-means on category unit shares, shrunk toward the average shopper by items so a small basket cannot look like a specialist. Five groups, chosen for reproducibility and stability rather than fit.

Assignment. Each customer's items are scored against every persona and against the average shopper, using a multinomial likelihood. The persona is given only when it beats the runner-up by times.

Channel. Treated as a separate attribute: store-first, web-first, app-first, or no single channel. In this data it is uncorrelated with persona, so the two carry different information.

Fit statistics are weak, and that is expected. The silhouette score is . Shopping behaviour has no natural gaps; segments are built, not discovered. Reproducibility, stability and prediction are the tests that matter.

Limits. Relevance is measured; the revenue effect of acting on it is not. Personas describe the past year and lapse when a customer buys less: of those labelled in 2025, still qualified in 2026.

Synthetic data. Maple & Main is fictional. Its customers were generated from five latent shopper types with varying strength, which is why the personas can be scored for accuracy. Figures illustrate the method, not any real company.

Code. SQL, Python and SAS, with a segmentation card and QC log, are in the project repository.
