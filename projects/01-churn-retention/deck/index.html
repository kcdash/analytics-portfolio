Defining churn

180 days of silence is the working definition, and it is wrong of the time

No order forFlagged as churnedFlagged who truly leftTrue leavers caught

Active customers on 3 November 2025. "Truly left" uses the hidden churn date in the synthetic data, which a real project would not have.

A short window flags quickly but mostly catches customers who were only between purchases. A long window is accurate but arrives too late to act.

180 days is the compromise: about six in ten flagged customers have really gone, and two thirds of real leavers are caught.

Infrequent buyers drive the false alarms. Treat a "churned" flag as a probability, not a fact, and never write customers off on the flag alone.

Across 19 campaigns with a full follow-up window, the difference is points, with a margin of error of . That is no measurable effect.

So this business case counts only the margin from the extra order in the 14-day response window. It assumes nothing about customers staying longer.

If retention is the goal, discounts are the wrong tool. The service data points somewhere else: complaints and unresolved issues are among the model's top warning signs, and they are fixable.

Business case

Same contacts, better list: more profit a year

Annual net profit from CRM offers, by how the audience is chosen

CAD. 25,000 contacts per campaign, 13 campaigns a year. Measured on nine held-out campaigns and scaled to a year. Bars show 95% intervals.

  • Ranking by churn risk is no better than picking at random. The difference is inside the margin of error.
  • Risk multiplied by customer value does just as well and needs no extra model. Start there.
  • The uplift model performs the same and adds one thing the simple ranking cannot: a do-not-contact list.
Honest scale: the gain is of annual margin. It is real and costs nothing to capture, but offers alone will not move retention.

Contacting more of the base would also add profit, since the average contact pays back. That depends on customers tolerating more frequent offers, which this data cannot show, so any volume increase should be tested with a holdout.

Appendix

Method, assumptions and limits

Population. Customers who joined at least 30 days before the snapshot and ordered in the prior 180 days.

Features. 33 inputs built in SQL from data dated before the snapshot: purchase recency, frequency and value, web activity, service contacts, CRM history, profile.

Validation. Train on four snapshots (Nov 2024 to Aug 2025), calibrate on Nov 2025, test on May 2026.

Uplift. Single gradient-boosting model with the treatment flag as an input, chosen over a two-model design on earlier campaigns. Predicted uplift tracks the true uplift built into the data (correlation ).

Back-test. Each targeting rule is scored only on measured treated-versus-holdout differences in nine campaigns the models never saw.

Assumed, not measured. Offer cost as a share of the responder's basket: 10% off 10%, bonus points 4%, $15 off $75 12%, free shipping 5%, win-back 20% off 20%. The simulator lets you change it.

Limits. Contacting the same top customers every month may tire them; this is not modelled. Loyalty tier is not point-in-time. Intervals reflect holdout sampling only.

Synthetic data. Maple & Main is fictional. The data was generated with known churn, uplift and media mechanisms, which is why results can be checked against ground truth. Figures illustrate the method, not any real company.

Code. SQL, Python and SAS, with a model card and QC log, are in the project repository.
